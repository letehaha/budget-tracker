Sometimes both sides of a transfer are already in MoneyMatter as separate transactions: an expense on one account and an income on another. This usually happens when both accounts are imported or connected to a bank. Linking the two turns them into one transfer, without deleting or changing either of them.

## Why link them

Until they're linked, MoneyMatter sees an ordinary expense and an ordinary income. Moving 800 from Checking to Savings then shows up as 800 spent **and** 800 earned, which inflates both totals.

Once linked, the pair is a transfer, and transfers are left out of income and expense totals. See [How transfers work](/transfers-and-refunds/how-transfers-work/#how-transfers-affect-stats).

You can link a pair from the transaction itself, or let **Transfer matching** find likely pairs for you.

<Aside type="note" title="Bank-connected accounts link most transfers for you">
Between your bank-connected accounts, MoneyMatter links most transfers for you after each sync. It does this when it finds exactly one match with the same amount and currency, in the opposite direction, within 3 days. Transfers between different currencies, and pairs with more than one possible match, are left for you to link here. If an automatic link is wrong, [unlink it](/transfers-and-refunds/how-transfers-work/#unlink-a-transfer). See [Bank connections](/import/bank-connections/). To also match against accounts you track by hand, turn on [Match transfers with manual accounts](/settings/general-settings/#match-transfers-with-manual-accounts).
</Aside>

## Link a pair from the transaction

<Steps>

1. Click the expense or the income in the transaction list to open **Edit Transaction**.
2. Select the **Transfer** tab.
3. In the **Where the money goes** panel, below the **or** divider, click **Link the other side**.
4. In **Find the other side of this transfer**, pick the matching transaction from the other account.
5. A **Linked transaction** row now shows your pick. Check it's the right one, then click **Edit** (the save button at the bottom of the dialog).

</Steps>

**Link the other side** only appears on a saved transaction that has no other side yet: an ordinary expense or income, or an [Out of Wallet transfer](/transfers-and-refunds/how-transfers-work/#out-of-wallet-transfers). It isn't there while you're adding a new transaction.

After you pick a transaction, the account and amount fields are locked, because both sides already have their own. To choose a different transaction, click the ✕ on the **Linked transaction** row and pick again.

<Aside type="caution" title="Link first, edit afterwards">
When you save with a linked transaction, MoneyMatter only saves the link. Any other change you made in the same dialog, such as a new note or date, is discarded. Link the pair first, then open the transfer again to edit it.
</Aside>

### What the list shows

**Find the other side of this transfer** has two sections.

- **Recommended** shows up to 7 likely matches: transactions on your other accounts whose amount, converted to your [base currency](/settings/base-currency-and-currencies/), is within 10% of this one, dated within 14 days either side. Transactions in the same currency come first, then the closest amounts, then the closest dates.
- **All transactions** lists the rest. To narrow it down, click the filter icon at the top of the dialog. It opens **Filters**, where you can set **From date**, **To date**, **Amount from** and **Amount to**.

Both sections only list transactions of the opposite type: incomes when you started from an expense, and the other way round. **Recommended** only lists transactions from your other accounts. When you start from an income, **All transactions** can also show expenses from the same account. Don't pick those, because linking them fails: the two sides must be on different accounts. **All transactions** can also include split and planned transactions, which can't be linked either. Picking one fails with "A transaction with splits can't be linked as a transfer." or "A planned transaction can't be linked as a transfer." Transfers, including Out of Wallet ones, and transactions linked as a refund aren't listed. Bank-connected accounts are included.

## Find pairs with Transfer matching

**Transfer matching** scans a date range for expense and income pairs that look like transfers between your accounts, and lets you link them one by one. It's handy after importing several months of history.

<Steps>

1. In the sidebar, open **Transactions**, then **Optimizations**.
2. Click the **Transfer matching** card.
3. Click the button with the calendar icon (it shows the current date range) and pick a range. Presets include **Last 3 months**, **Last 6 months**, **Last year**, **This year** and **Previous year**. The page starts on the last three months.
4. Optionally tick **Include out-of-wallet transfers**. See [Out of Wallet transactions](#out-of-wallet-transactions).
5. Click **Scan**.

</Steps>

When the scan finishes, you'll see how many potential transfers were found.

- The left column lists the expenses that have at least one possible match, newest first, each with its number of matches.
- The right column, **Suggestions for**, shows up to 4 incomes that could be the other side of the selected expense. Each has a confidence score and two buttons, **Skip** and **Link**. On a narrow screen, the suggestions open in a dialog instead.

Click the expense under **Suggestions for**, or any suggested income, to open it in **Edit Transaction**. Clicking an expense in the left column only selects it. When you close the dialog, the page scans again so the results stay up to date.

### How pairs are found

For each expense in the date range, MoneyMatter looks for incomes that:

- are on a **different account**;
- are within **10%** of the expense's amount, after converting both to your base currency;
- are dated within **14 days** of the expense. Incomes up to 14 days outside the chosen range count too, so a transfer that straddles the edge of the range is still found.

Transactions that are already transfers, are linked as a refund, or are split across categories are left out on both sides.

### The confidence score

The score is out of 100 and is shown as a percentage. It adds up three things:

- up to **50 points** for how close the two amounts are;
- up to **30 points** for how close the two dates are;
- **20 points** if both transactions are in the same currency.

The dot next to the score is green at 75% or more, amber from 50%, and red below 50%.

### Link a suggestion

Click **Link** next to the income you want. The **Link as transfer?** dialog shows which account the money went from and to, and both amounts. Click **Link** to confirm. You'll see "Transactions linked as transfer".

### Skip a suggestion

Click **Skip** if a suggestion isn't a transfer, for example a purchase and a salary that happen to be the same amount. Confirm in **Skip this suggestion?**. You can tick **Don't show this confirmation again** to skip without asking next time, in this browser.

<Aside type="caution" title="Skipping can't be undone">
A skipped pair is hidden from Transfer matching for good. Skipping only affects this page: both transactions stay as they are, and you can still link them from the transaction itself with **Link the other side**, where skipped pairs still show up.
</Aside>

### Out of Wallet transactions

By default, the scan leaves out transactions recorded as **Out of Wallet**. Tick **Include out-of-wallet transfers** to include them. This is useful when you recorded one side as going to an account you didn't track, and you've since added that account. Linking turns the Out of Wallet transaction into a regular transfer between your two accounts.

## Rules for linking

Whichever way you link, the two transactions must be:

- **one expense and one income**;
- **on different accounts**;
- **not already a transfer**. An Out of Wallet transaction is the exception: linking turns it into a transfer between your accounts;
- **not split across categories**. Linking a split transaction fails with "A transaction with splits can't be linked as a transfer." Remove the splits first;
- **not planned**.

The amounts, currencies and dates **don't have to match**. That's on purpose: a transfer between currencies has two different amounts, and a bank transfer can take a few days to arrive.

## What linking changes

Only the pairing. Both transactions keep their amounts, dates, notes and categories, and account balances don't move, because each side was already counted on its own account. What changes is that the pair now counts as a transfer, so it drops out of your income and expense totals.

A linked transfer then behaves like any other transfer. You can [edit it](/transfers-and-refunds/how-transfers-work/#edit-a-transfer), and deleting one side [deletes both](/transfers-and-refunds/how-transfers-work/#delete-a-transfer).

<Aside type="note" title="Good to know">
When neither side came from a bank connection, the next time you edit and save the transfer, the date, note and payment type in the form are written to both sides. If the two sides had different dates, both end up with the date of the **From account** side (the expense).
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Saving can change the income's amount">
When neither side came from a bank connection and both use the same currency, saving the transfer also sets the income's amount to the expense's **Amount**, even if they differed, for example because of a fee. This changes the destination account's balance. To keep two different amounts, don't save the transfer from **Edit Transaction**.
</Aside>

## Unlink a pair

To turn a linked pair back into an ordinary expense and income, open it and click **Unlink transactions** in the **Where the money goes** panel. Both transactions go back to being ordinary ones and keep their current amounts, dates and categories. See [Unlink a transfer](/transfers-and-refunds/how-transfers-work/#unlink-a-transfer).
