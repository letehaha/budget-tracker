One supermarket receipt can cover food, cleaning supplies and a birthday present. A split lets you keep it as one transaction, matching your bank statement, while each part counts toward the right category.

## How a split works

Every split transaction has a **main category**, the one in the **Category** field. You then add split rows, each with its own category and amount. **The main category keeps whatever isn't split off.**

Say you spend 100 EUR at a supermarket, with **Shopping** as the main category, and split off:

- Groceries: 30 EUR
- Drug-store, chemist: 20 EUR

The transaction still shows as one 100 EUR expense, and your account balance drops by 100 EUR. In your stats it counts as:

| Category | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Groceries | 30 EUR |
| Drug-store, chemist | 20 EUR |
| Shopping (main category) | 50 EUR |

Reports grouped by top-level category add each subcategory into its parent, so there Drug-store, chemist counts toward Shopping (70 EUR) and Groceries toward Food & Drinks (30 EUR).

So the split amounts don't have to add up to the total. You only list the parts that belong somewhere else.

## Split a transaction

<Steps>

1. Open a new or existing expense or income.
2. Enter the **Amount** and pick the main **Category** first. The split window works out what's left from the amount.
3. Click **Split** on the right of the **Category** field. The **Split into categories** window opens.
4. In the first row, pick a category and enter its amount.
5. Click **Add category** for each extra part.
6. Check the summary at the bottom: **Total amount**, **Split total** and **Main category keeps**.
7. Click **Save splits**.
8. Click **Create**, or **Edit** if you're changing an existing transaction (that's the save button's label). **The splits aren't saved until you do.**

</Steps>

## Rules for split rows

- **Each amount must be more than zero.**
- **Together, the splits can't be more than the transaction amount.** The window shows **Split amounts exceed transaction total** and won't let you save. Splits that add up to exactly the total are fine; the main category then keeps nothing.
- **Each category can be used once.** A repeat shows **Duplicate category**.
- **The main category can't be a split row.** It already gets the remainder, so the window shows **Cannot use main category in splits**.
- **Up to 10 split rows** per transaction.
- Rows with no category or no amount are dropped when you save.

<Aside type="tip" title="Save splits is greyed out?">
Check that the transaction has an **Amount**. Without it, there's nothing to split, so what the main category keeps goes below zero and the window won't save.
</Aside>

## Change or remove splits

To change splits, open the transaction, click the **Split into … categories** row under **Category**, adjust the rows, click **Save splits**, then save the transaction.

To remove all splits, open the same window and click **Remove splits**. The whole amount goes back to the main category once you save the transaction. To drop only one part, click **Remove category** on that row.

Deleting the transaction deletes its splits too.

## How splits show up

- **In stats and reports**, each split counts under its own category and the main category counts what's left, as in the [example above](#how-a-split-works). See [how stats are calculated](/stats/how-stats-are-calculated/#split-transactions).
- **In the transaction list**, a split transaction shows a split icon in list view. In table view, turn on the **Split** column in **Table settings** to see it.
- **When you filter by category**, a split transaction appears if its main category or any of its split categories matches. The whole transaction is listed, not only the matching part. See [search and filter](/transactions/search-and-filter/#available-filters).
- **In another currency**, each split is converted to your base currency with the same exchange rate as the rest of the transaction, the one for its date.

## Limits and side effects

- **Transfers can't be split.** A transfer moves money between your accounts, so it doesn't belong to spending categories.
- **Switching a split transaction to Transfer deletes its splits** when you save, with no warning. For the same reason, a split transaction can't be linked to another transaction as a transfer.
- **Split transactions can't be selected for bulk editing.** Edit them one at a time.
- **Tags, payee and note belong to the whole transaction.** In the split window, each row only has a category and an amount.
- **Bank-synced transactions can be split**, even though their amount is locked.

## Splits and refunds

When you link a refund to a split transaction, you can choose which part it refunds: the main category or one of the splits. The refund then reduces that category in your stats. See [refunds](/transfers-and-refunds/refunds/).

A few rules follow from that:

- You can't lower a split's amount below what's already been refunded against it. Unlink the refund first if you need to.
- If you remove a split that has a refund linked to it, the refund is linked to the whole transaction instead.
